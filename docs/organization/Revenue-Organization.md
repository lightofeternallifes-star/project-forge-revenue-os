# PROJECT FORGE Revenue Organization™

## Operating Model

PROJECT FORGE Revenue OS™ is organized as an enterprise Revenue Organization rather than a collection of channel automations. The organization owns strategy, intelligence, relationships, decisions, evidence, risk, and outcomes across the complete revenue lifecycle.

## Leadership Levels

- **CEO:** owns company strategy, mission, capital allocation, and final enterprise accountability.
- **Executive Leadership:** owns cross-department strategy, risk appetite, customer trust, investment, and operating cadence.
- **Department:** owns one domain, its canonical data, policies, KPIs, evidence, and interfaces.
- **Director:** owns a department portfolio, standards, staffing, capacity, and cross-team outcomes.
- **Manager:** owns a bounded operating area, queues, quality, escalation, and daily execution.
- **Digital Employee:** performs bounded, versioned work under explicit capability and approval policy.
- **Specialist:** provides expert judgment, review, research, investigation, or customer context.
- **Worker:** performs repeatable, documented tasks under supervision and quality controls.

## Single-Owner Rule

Every responsibility, canonical entity, policy, KPI, evidence class, and escalation decision has exactly one accountable department. Other departments may contribute inputs, consume outputs, or review decisions, but cannot silently become co-owners.

## Enterprise Operating Cadence

- Daily: queue health, customer risk, security events, escalations, and critical revenue changes.
- Weekly: department KPIs, evidence quality, Digital Employee performance, provider health, and blocked work.
- Monthly: executive operating review, financial impact, learning results, policy changes, and audit exceptions.
- Quarterly: strategy, organizational capacity, risk appetite, architecture evolution, and budget allocation.

## Scale Model

The hierarchy is logical and can serve one customer or millions. Organizations, departments, Digital Employees, workflows, evidence, and permissions are tenant-scoped and versioned. Physical teams, queues, regions, and work pools may scale independently without changing the canonical organization model.
